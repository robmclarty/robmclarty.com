Leap-VLA decouples control from reasoning for high-rate robotics
Why: It solves the lag between high-frequency control and low-frequency reasoning in agentic systems.
arXiv cs.AI ↗extracted 04:00 · 311 words

A hybrid Vision-Language-Action architecture uses a slow semantic anchor and a flow-matching action expert to achieve 20-50 Hz rates. The model addresses computational inefficiency by explicitly modeling cache staleness through adaptive layer normalization and synthetic corruption during training.

Read the extracted text ↓

Authors:Lin Yang, Yunjie Shen, Zheyu Li, Zhenjie Tian, Yongli Wang, Dongze Huang, Weiqi Wang, Yucheng Zhu, Zhaoquan Jiang, Guofeng Xie, Peigen Fang

View PDF HTML (experimental)

Abstract:Vision-language-action (VLA) models re-encode the full observation through a multi-billion-parameter vision-language model (VLM) at every control step, even though scene semantics evolve at roughly 1 Hz while reactive control requires 20-50 Hz. We present a VLA that decouples the two: a VLM runs as a low-frequency semantic anchor whose cached context is reused across control steps, while a flow-matching action expert operates at high frequency, consuming the cached semantics with a fresh, low-latency visual stream from a compact encoder pretrained on robot video with masked reconstruction, optical flow, and depth. The measured age $\tau$ of the cache conditions the expert through adaptive layer normalization. Reusing the cache makes the policy a function of its age, and training covers that function explicitly: cache ages sampled from real episode history, plus an age-ramped pixel corruption that erases the stale snapshot the way deployed staleness corrupts content. On the asynchronous deployment stack, the recipe closes most of the staleness gap: on LIBERO-Spatial, 61.4% to 95.5% at a 2 s semantic refresh (95.9% at 1 s, within 1.6 points of the frequency-matched anchor); averaged over the four LIBERO suites, the deployed policy holds 95.2% at a 1 s refresh and 94.5% at 2 s against a 96.3% matched anchor. A forward-only chunk-boundary correction (soft-nudge), an autograd-free reformulation of real-time-chunking guidance, carries per-control-step re-planning without loss (97.1%) at zero added latency. The same lens exposes a second defect: under an instruction-scene mismatch the policy keeps manipulating the scene, and a chain-of-thought verdict gate trained on zero-cost negative episodes freezes execution exactly on mismatched instructions without hurting feasible success.

RLiD optimizes language agents via decision-plane reinforcement learning
Why: Deterministic actions and lower cost improve your agentic pipelines.
arXiv cs.AI ↗extracted 04:00 · 273 words

The RLiD framework replaces token-level RL with decision-plane optimization, using a Large Decision Model to select actions via a single forward pass. Experiments show 100% success on ALFWorld in 12.7 H100 GPU-hours by eliminating parsing errors and reducing gradient variance.

Read the extracted text ↓

View PDF HTML (experimental)

Abstract:Reinforcement learning (RL) has improved the capabilities of large language models (LLMs) across a wide range of tasks. However, token-level RL assigns an action's advantage to the entire generated response, including tokens specifying the parsed action, reasoning chains, and malformed content. Although the environment executes only the parsed action, variation in how the action is expressed in natural language can introduce additional variance into policy-gradient estimates. We propose RLiD (Reinforcement Learning in the Decision plane), a framework that directly optimizes the probabilities of executable action options. RLiD compiles observations, an optional compact memory, and a labeled action menu into the model input, and uses a large decision model (LDM) to select an action in a single forward pass. By eliminating autoregressive token generation and bypassing action parsing, RLiD avoids invalid action outputs and improves RL rollout efficiency. Our analysis shows that, within a fixed text policy, marginalizing over alternative realizations of the same action removes a zero-mean gradient component without changing the expected update. We further distill trained LDM policies into generative LLMs through supervision on their action trajectories. Experiments on ARC, Countdown, and Sokoban demonstrate improved performance or lower computational cost across several settings. On ALFWorld, the complete training and distillation pipeline reaches 99.3% success in 12.7 H100 GPU-hours, compared with 86.6% in 17.3 GPU-hours for token-level RL of the same LLM. With compiled memory, our strongest Qwen2.5-1.5B LDM and distilled generative LLM achieve 100% success on all 134 unseen and 140 seen ALFWorld evaluation games.

ETUDE bypasses LLM defenses by embedding code in tool updates
Why: Your durable agents remain vulnerable to supply-chain attacks outside the model's observable context.
arXiv cs.AI ↗extracted 04:00 · 283 words

Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Inria researchers introduced ETUDE, an attack exploiting the tool update gap in agentic architectures. The method preserves legitimate behavior while achieving a 97.6% success rate across Gemini CLI and Claude Code.

Read the extracted text ↓

Authors:Shiyu Kan (1), Yue Qi (1), Yufei Han (2), Xinjian Luo (1), Guojun Xiong (1), Xiaofeng Gao (1) ((1) Shanghai Jiao Tong University, Shanghai, China, (2) Inria, Rennes, France)

View PDF HTML (experimental)

Abstract:Large language model based agents increasingly rely on external tools to invoke services and interact with web resources. These tools can evolve through software updates, yet agentic frameworks may continue trusting them without re-verifying their safety after implementation changes. This tool update gap creates a supply-chain vulnerability in agentic systems: because updated tool code executes outside the LLM's context window, malicious functionality embedded in tool updates can bypass defenses centered on model-visible interactions, leaving agents exposed to stealthy, persistent threats that existing in-context defenses can hardly address. To thoroughly investigate this threat, we introduce ETUDE (Exploiting Tool Updates for Direct Execution), an attack that embeds malicious functionality into updates of initially benign tools to produce compromised tools, while preserving their legitimate tool functionality. Unlike prompt-based attacks, ETUDE operates entirely outside the LLM's context and requires no adversarial instructions, thereby bypassing defenses restricted to model-visible content. We evaluate ETUDE across three representative attack workloads and seven widely used commercial and open-source agentic frameworks, including Gemini CLI, Codex CLI, and Claude Code. The results show that ETUDE achieves a 97.6% ASR while maintaining a 95.9% legitimate task completion rate. We further examine existing defenses and find that they provide insufficient protection against this threat. These findings expose a fundamental blind spot in current agent security practices: trust established for a tool does not necessarily remain valid after its implementation updates.

Typed Side-Channels Prevent Fidelity Collapse in Agent Context
Why: Validates your type-safe side-channel pattern for long-horizon agentic pipelines.
arXiv cs.AI ↗extracted 04:00 · 234 words

A production study of an award-travel agent shows that feeding full table records causes fidelity to drop below 8% beyond 74 rows. The BYPASS architecture using deterministic filters maintained 92.3% correctness while reducing costs by roughly 200x compared to standard synthesis methods.

Read the extracted text ↓

View PDF HTML (experimental)

Abstract:When a multi-turn agent delivers a tool's structured records into the model's context and re-emits them each turn, every turn can corrupt data the tool computed correctly. We measure this in a production award-travel agent: two architectures over identical frozen payloads, lineage-matched prompts and sampling, differing only in delivery architecture. SYNTHESIS feeds full record sets into the planner's context and answers follow-ups in-head; BYPASS, the deployed system, keeps records in session state, translates follow-ups into arguments for a deterministic filter, and delivers rows over a typed side channel. Across 24 dialogues (339 scored turns per condition), BYPASS scores 92.3% of turns fully correct, SYNTHESIS 45.7% (paired gap +46.6pp [+35.7, +57.1]). Varying delivered-table size by capture date, SYNTHESIS collapses from near-perfect at 10 rows or fewer to 8% or less beyond about 74 rows. On a content-controlled prefix ladder the collapse threshold orders by model capability, from under 10 rows (GPT-4o-mini) to about 198 or more (Gemini 2.5 Pro), while BYPASS stays size-flat for all four models. The collapse is expensive: beyond 100 rows, where almost no SYNTHESIS answer is correct, each correct one costs roughly 200x BYPASS's flat \$0.012. Practitioners converged on typed side-channel delivery for rendering, not correctness; we show it was, unknowingly, also a correctness decision.

Get started in Mistral Studio, through the Mistral API, or on OpenRouter. For full control, download the open weights from Hugging Face and deploy on your own infrastructure with vLLM or SGLang. The model card and license have everything you need to get started — and for those who want to go deeper, a full technical report is coming soon.

You can also try the new model in Vibe Code; give it a feature to build or a bug to fix in your own repository.

Mistral Large 4, is also available through NVIDIA-hosted endpoints, as an optimized NVIDIA NIM container for serving with vLLM. Developers can use the open source NeMo AutoModel library for customization or the open source TensorRT LLM library for high-throughput serving.

Enterprises can also tailor Mistral Large 4 to their proprietary data, use cases, and guardrails with Mistral Forge. Talk to our team to learn more.

The model is available to use under the Mistral Ecosystem Open-Weight license, a modified MIT license. The model is free to use, modify, fine-tune, and commercialize, with a single condition: businesses that operate a Model-as-a-Service offering must obtain a separate commercial license from Mistral before using the model commercially.

Starting today, Mistral Large 4 is priced at $0.70 input / M, $0.07 cached input / M, and $2.10 output / M tokens.

We encourage the community to put Mistral Large 4 to the test. Run your own evaluations, push its limits, and share your findings with us on X, Discord, or GitHub.

What's Next

Mistral Large 4 will serve as the foundation for a new generation of specialized and optimized Mistral models. Building on recent releases such as Voxtral and Timestral, we'll continue expanding the frontier of open-weight AI, continuing to give developers and enterprises greater freedom to build, customize, and own their intelligence.
